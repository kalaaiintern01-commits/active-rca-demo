The whole path, once

Six stages. Every arrow is data moving; nothing moves right to left.

The important structural fact: data only moves one way. The ESP32 senses and reports. It has no motor-driver output, no control loop, no authority over the machine. Two settings can arrive from outside — a fault label and the publish interval — and neither touches hardware.

01

Four sensors, four completely different ways of reading them

A digital bus, a register bus, an analogue pin, and an interrupt.

People assume "reading a sensor" is one skill. It isn't. These four sensors share a microcontroller and nothing else: one talks over a single wire with microsecond timing, one over a two-wire register bus, one is just a voltage, and one doesn't get "read" at all — it interrupts the CPU.

Temperature

1-Wire · GPIO4 DS18B20 12-bit · 0.0625 °C 1 Hz

The DS18B20 is a small computer, not a thermistor. You send it a command, it converts internally, and you read back a scratchpad. The catch is that a 12-bit conversion takes 750 ms — and the obvious way to write this code destroys everything else.

Why the range check matters more than it looks

A disconnected DS18B20 returns −127 °C. If that reaches the cloud as a number, the model learns −127 °C as a legitimate machine state, and a real sensor failure later looks normal. So the payload carries temp.valid, and a bad reading is marked rather than shipped.

Vibration

I²C 400 kHz · GPIO21/22 MPU6050 ±8 g 94 Hz DLPF 200 Hz

An accelerometer at rest does not read zero. It reads 9.81 m/s² — gravity. Average its output and you learn which way is down, not how much the motor is shaking. Gravity is 20 to 100 times larger than the vibration we care about, so it has to go first.

Then: three numbers out of 400 samples

From the residual we keep RMS (how much energy), peak (the largest single excursion), and their ratio, crest factor — which is the cheapest fault classifier in the whole project:

crest = peak / RMS   →   ≈ 1.41 smooth sine — rotor imbalance, one heavy spot going round
                        > 3    sharp impacts — a bearing ball striking a crack, once per pass

Two faults with identical RMS, told apart by the shape of the signal, from a sensor that costs about ₹150.

Why the filter is set to 94 Hz

Nyquist's theorem: sampling at 200 Hz, you can only represent signals up to 100 Hz. Feed in a 150 Hz vibration and it doesn't get ignored — it reappears disguised as 50 Hz. That's aliasing, and it is poison for a model: a completely fictional feature it will happily learn from. The MPU6050's internal low-pass filter is set to 94 Hz so out-of-band energy is removed before it can be sampled and mislabelled. Same reason a car wheel appears to spin backwards on video.

Current

ADC1 · GPIO34 ACS712, Hall effect 100 mV/A 200 Hz

The simplest electrical interface and the fussiest calibration. The ACS712 sits in series with the motor supply; motor current flows through an internal conductor and a Hall element measures the magnetic field it creates. The sense side is galvanically isolated, which is why a 12 V motor circuit cannot hurt a 3.3 V microcontroller through it.

Its output is a voltage centred on half its supply: 2.5 V means zero amps, and every 100 mV away from that is one amp, in either direction.

Why RMS and not the average

Motor current is not smooth DC — it pulses with commutation, and under an AC drive it alternates around zero, where the plain average is ≈ 0 A even at full load. RMS squares each sample first, which makes negatives positive and weights large excursions more heavily. That is not a trick: heating in the winding goes as I²R, so RMS is the DC-equivalent heating value — the number that actually predicts thermal ageing.

Speed

Interrupt · GPIO27 optical / Hall tacho 2 pulses/rev event-driven

This one is never "read". A slot or magnet passing the sensor produces a pulse, the pulse yanks a GPIO high, and the CPU stops whatever it is doing to run six lines of code. That is an interrupt service routine, and it is why no pulse is ever missed even while the main loop is busy with Wi-Fi.

Two speeds come out of those two variables, and they answer different questions:

rpm.avg  = pulses × 60000 / (window_ms × pulses_per_rev)   — steady, averaged over 2 s; this is what you trend
rpm.inst = 60000000 / (last_period_us × pulses_per_rev)   — reacts within a single pulse; catches transients

When those two disagree by more than about 1%, something is wrong with the measurement — and that is exactly how a real 3% error in this firmware got caught during testing.

03

400 samples become 15 numbers

Averaging on the device removes noise before it costs bandwidth.

Nothing is stored. Each sample is folded into four running totals and discarded — so summarising 400 samples costs a handful of bytes of RAM, not an array.

The bandwidth argument, with numbers

raw, every sample    200 Hz × 8 bytes = 1600 B/s → 138 MB per day, per motor
summarised            540 B every 2 s = 270 B/s → 23 MB per day

Six times less — and that is against a raw binary stream. As JSON, per-sample would be fifty times worse. But the saving is the lesser half of the argument: the summary is also the part the model actually wants. RMS, peak and crest factor are the features. The individual samples were only ever raw material.

04

What actually gets sent

One document per window, ~540 bytes, on a versioned schema.

Roughly a third of those fields are not measurements. They are there so the receiver can decide whether to believe the measurements — which is the difference between a data stream and a trustworthy one.

FieldJobWhy it exists
schemaversion tag When the payload changes shape next year, v2 runs alongside v1 and nothing breaks.
seqmonotonic counter A gap means a message was lost. Loss becomes visible instead of invisible.
ts_syncedclock honesty false means NTP hasn't synced; the cloud should use arrival time, not a fabricated timestamp.
t_edge_mspublish instant Subtract it at the dashboard to report a measured end-to-end latency rather than an estimate.
labelfault annotation Set over MQTT while a fault is induced. This is what turns a stream of numbers into a labelled training set.
temp.validreading trust A failed sensor is marked, never forwarded as −127 °C for the model to learn.
cur.cal_okcalibration trust An implausible zero point invalidates the whole current channel. Better to say so than to publish a confident wrong number.
win.msreal window length The actual measured window, so the cloud never has to assume it was exactly 2000 ms.
win.n / droppedcoverage Expected 400, got 398? That's a quality gate: a degraded window can be excluded rather than silently trusted.
net.*link health Falling RSSI and rising reconnect counts explain missing data after the fact.
05

How MQTT gets it off the device

Publish/subscribe: the device announces, it never sends to anyone.

With HTTP, a client asks a specific server for a specific thing and waits for an answer. MQTT inverts that. The ESP32 publishes to a named topic on a broker. Anyone interested subscribes to that topic. The device does not know how many subscribers exist — or whether there are any at all.

That decoupling is the point. Add a dashboard, a second database, an alerting service: the firmware never changes. And the protocol overhead is a 2-byte header against HTTP's hundreds of bytes of text, which matters when you are doing it every two seconds on battery-adjacent hardware.

The topic tree

TopicDirectionPayloadNotes
pdm/v1/MOTOR-01/telemetryout ~540 B JSONevery 2 s, QoS 0
pdm/v1/MOTOR-01/statusout online / offline retained, and the last-will message
pdm/v1/MOTOR-01/cmdin {"label":"…"} label, publish interval, re-zero

The v1 sits in the path, not just in the payload, so a future firmware revision can publish a different shape to a different topic while old subscribers keep working.

Four protocol features doing real work

FeatureSettingWhat it buys
QoS 0at most once Telemetry is periodic and loss-tolerant — the next window is 2 s away. Higher QoS costs round-trips and buffering for data that is about to be superseded. seq makes any loss visible, which is cheaper than preventing it.
Retainedstatus only The broker keeps the last status message. Open the dashboard an hour later and it immediately knows whether the device is up, without waiting for an event.
Last will"offline" Distinguishes “nothing has changed” from “the device is gone” — the two look identical otherwise.
Keepalive30 s Silence longer than this and the broker declares the client dead and fires the will. Sets how quickly a failure is noticed.

When the network fails, which it will

Wi-Fi association and the broker handshake block for over a second, so the window spanning them has poor sample coverage. The firmware therefore realigns the window boundary on connect — the first window it publishes is a complete one, not a partial one dressed up as complete.

TLS is a build switch: USE_TLS 1 moves to port 8883. In the lab the broker is accepted without pinning a certificate; on the cloud broker in Objective 2 that becomes a pinned root CA plus a per-device certificate and private key, so the broker knows which motor is talking and nobody can impersonate it.

06

How the stream tells you when not to trust it

The design rule: never publish a wrong number as if it were right.

A model trained on data that silently lies is worse than no model, because it is confidently wrong. So every failure mode has a field, and each one is a gate the cloud can act on rather than a surprise later.

What goes wrongHow you find outWhat the cloud does
Temperature sensor unpluggedtemp.valid = false Ignore the channel; alert on the sensor, not the motor
Current zero drifted or motor ran at bootcur.cal_ok = false Reject the current channel entirely for this device
Loop starved, samples missedwin.n < sample_hz × ms Exclude a window below ~95% coverage from training
Message lost in transitgap in seq Interpolate or skip — knowingly, not accidentally
Clock not yet syncedts_synced = false Use broker arrival time instead of the device timestamp
Device powered off or crashedstatus = "offline" Show the device as stale rather than showing old numbers
Payload larger than the bufferpublish_fails climbs Nothing truncated is ever sent — half a JSON document is worse than none

This paid for itself during testing

win.ms and win.n exist purely as diagnostics. They are what revealed that windows were 1940 ms instead of 2000, that 3% of samples were being discarded, and that RPM was consequently 3% high. Then win.dropped pinned the remaining loss on the serial monitor: printing 540 bytes of JSON at 115200 baud blocks the loop for 47 ms, which is 9 samples, every window. Neither bug was visible in the sensor readings themselves.
07

Where this stops

Objective 1 ends at the broker, deliberately.

Nothing in this firmware decides anything. There is no health score, no anomaly detection, no threshold that raises an alarm. That is not an omission — it is the architecture. The edge senses and communicates; the cloud predicts; a deterministic rule engine decides.

The reason is practical. If the model lived on the ESP32, improving it would mean reflashing hardware bolted to a running machine. Keeping analytics in the cloud means the device stays cheap, simple, and rarely touched — and because prediction and decision are separate, every future alert traces back to a specific inspectable condition rather than to a number nobody can audit.
